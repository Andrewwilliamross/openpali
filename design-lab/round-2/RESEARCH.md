# OpenPali / round two: a subject worth remembering

Independent brand review, September 26, 2026. Research and art direction only.
No new visual direction is implemented or selected by this document.

## Assessment

The first round explored rendering techniques more convincingly than it
developed an identity. Six landing pages mostly repeat the same composition:
large left-hand type, an isolated object on the right, one blue action, and a
small civic-purpose sentence. The object changes from letters to sheets to
cubes to glass. The imagined world stays almost empty.

The rejected P should leave the center of the next exploration. Making it more
beautiful, physical, or interactive would continue an idea the user has
already rejected. A replacement mascot also needs a setting and a job. Putting
a bird where the P stood would preserve the same weakness.

For the next round, define **a place, an activity, and a recurring point of
view** before choosing an image generator, Blender, or Three.js. The visual
subject should help explain why someone would care about public records in the
Palisades. Craft and technology can then make that subject memorable.

## What was actually inspected

The research used a real browser at 1440 × 1000 to inspect the supplied
[Cua website](https://cua.ai/), its settled hero, the fleet section, the official
[branding page](https://cua.ai/branding), and the rendered
[GitHub README](https://github.com/trycua/cua), including two different frames
of the animated product cards. Screenshots were opened as images. The
[README source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/trycua/cua/main/README.md)
confirmed how those illustrated cards are arranged and linked.

I also reopened the completed OpenPali gallery capture and compared it with
the concept screenshots reviewed in round one. This is an internal design
judgment, not audience research or a claim about Cua's conversion results.

artwork, and specific styling are reference material; they should not be
recreated for OpenPali.

### What to learn, and what to leave with Cua

The useful lesson is a recurring subject that can act. OpenPali can have its
own actors and artifacts: a source document, a field annotation, a folding
map, an unanswered question, a correction, or a published edition. Each can
reappear with a different purpose while retaining a recognizable visual
language.

Copying the rounded animal face, dark glowing stage, exact serif/sans/mono
combination, or a computer full of particles would import Cua's answers. Our
white-and-blue constraint remains useful. What is missing is the specific
life happening inside that palette.

## A stricter audit of round one

| Study | What remains useful | Why it falls short of this new brief |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fieldnotes | Reading order, restrained rules, concise civic purpose. | An editorial layout is not yet an editorial world. There are no illustrations, recurring subjects, or recognizably local details. |
| Ledger | Source, observation, and release give it the strongest semantic basis. | Tiny generic cards and a depth slider turn a meaningful relationship into an abstract interface demonstration. The scene needs a record with an understandable story. |
| Assembly | Visitors can directly manipulate the artwork. | Rearranging a P teaches little about this project. The same behavior could introduce almost any product with that initial. |
| Signal | Cobalt has impact at small social-image sizes. | A large P in a square field remains a graphic treatment, even when the squares move. The palette is doing most of the identification. |
| Material | Real craft, repeatable source geometry, useful export discipline. | Glass and porcelain communicate care, but the object could advertise storage, finance, or furniture. Its connection to civic evidence depends on the caption. |
| Aperture | Quiet composition and a controlled optical image. | Transparency is a broad metaphor used by many products. The image has little narrative content to extend into a community or a README. |

The strongest reusable pieces are infrastructure: accessible controls,
responsive layouts, local assets, clear copy, and the habit of visual review.
They should support new subjects rather than determine them.

## Three different visual stories

These are deliberately different in setting, drawing method, composition, and
visitor action. They should be presented as three finished art studies before
one becomes a full website and repository system.

### A. The Shared Table

**Community and character direction.** A small, open field office where people
bring records and work on a common picture.

**The opening scene**

A long worktable runs diagonally across a white page. We see it from the
height of someone about to sit down, rather than from the usual floating
isometric angle. On it: a large folded coastal map, blue pencils worn to
different lengths, a ruler, an archive box, and three documents held together
by a conspicuously long source tab. One chair is pulled out toward the viewer.
An open shade canopy and a drawn ridge beyond it establish an outdoor working
place. There are no damaged homes, construction promises, or official seals.

At one end, an original, slender shorebird-like character tries to manage a
map larger than itself. Working name: **Footnote**. Its long beak, small feet,
asymmetric posture, and ruled-paper wing distinguish it from a round plush
mascot. Its personality is attentive and slightly overprepared. It carries a
source tag even when the sheet is comically oversized. A single blue knot in
its document strap is enough recurring costume detail.

The character represents the project's care with records. It never represents
a survivor, impersonates a public employee, or decides what happened to a
household. The invitation comes from the spare chair and shared task. The
character adds a small, repeatable human moment to it.

**Art direction**

Use original cobalt ink drawing with confident irregular contours and flat
white shapes. Concentrate detail in objects people handle; leave the sky and
background open. Avoid toy rendering and uniform rounded corners. The map
provides a restrained geographic cue, with any precise geography checked
against an appropriate source during implementation. The initial study is an
illustration, not a data map.

**One visitor action**

Hover, focus, or tap one loose document. Footnote catches its lifting corner;
the document's source tab stays attached and a short annotation becomes
readable. The motion ends. The same information is available without motion.
This is an illustration interaction beside the single GitHub action, with no
form, account, or demand to finish a game.

**Recurring scenes and motifs**

- README introduction: the full table and open chair, cropped into a wide
  banner with the project description in ordinary text below it.
- Contribution card: Footnote makes room for a new sheet; a second pencil
  enters from the edge of the frame. Caption: “There is room for careful work.”
- Sources card: the character carries one record and its attached reference,
  even as a breeze makes that inconvenient.
- Corrections card: a penciled annotation is added beside the original;
  the original remains visible.
- Release card: a tidy edition wrapper around several sheets. The edition
  number is real release metadata when such an artifact is published.
- Small assets: a chair, source tab, pencil, map fold, and the character's
  unmistakable side profile. The project mark can remain small.

**Tone and provisional copy**

“A place at the table.”

“Public evidence for the Palisades rebuild. Bring a source, a question, or a
careful improvement.”

The humor comes from ordinary working details: an oversized map, a pencil
that rolls, the character refusing to lose a footnote. It stays away from
loss, waiting for permits, or the pace of recovery.

**Why it is specific**

The central action joins fragmented records, preserves their source, and
leaves room for correction. The open seat makes participation visible. A
verified coastal/ridge setting places the activity in the Palisades. Those
elements must survive even when the wordmark is removed.

**Risk and the design response**

A field office could look like an aid station or government service. Keep the
setting informal, identify the independent project in nearby copy, and omit
uniforms, official-looking stamps, and service promises. A bird alone could
be generic startup decoration. Approve it only in at least three different
record-related scenes. If it cannot sustain those scenes, discard it.

### B. The Folded Coast

**Editorial and illustrative direction.** A public atlas whose folds and
annotations explain how a shared picture is assembled.

**The opening scene**

The browser opens on a large, unfolded sheet that crosses the page edge. A
drawn coastal landscape occupies most of the composition: water, a bluff,
roads climbing into a ridge, and a few ordinary civic details. The drawing
uses a strong horizontal coastal line with an occasional blue wash. A large
folio title sits in an actual margin, and numbered notes point to details in
the drawing. This is a composed illustration with a sense of place, not a
dashboard or a stock aerial photo.

One fold is partly open. Inside it, the same record appears in three readable
forms: a source fragment, a plain-language observation, and the published
context. They occupy real areas of the sheet rather than tiny floating cards.
Use an explicitly illustrative example until verified source material has
been selected and its reproduction terms checked.

**Art direction**

Use white paper, deep blue linework, pale blue ink, generous margins, and an
editorial serif whose drawing differs from Cua's narrow display face. Let
handwritten marks appear only where a person is making a note. Main text stays
typeset and readable. Flat illustration, crop changes, and paper folds do the
visual work; drop shadows remain minimal.

The exact Palisades geography must be deliberately researched for the chosen
image. A generic sea, palm tree, and mountain would weaken the premise.
Names, contour shapes, and sightlines should have identifiable references.
No illustrated property receives an invented recovery status.

**One visitor action**

Open the partly folded margin. A continuous annotation line follows a claim
back to its source. Closing it returns to the whole landscape. The interaction
reveals a relationship that exists in the product, and works as a simple
keyboard-operable disclosure. It does not animate rebuilding houses or fill
the map with success colors.

**Recurring scenes and motifs**

- README banner: a wide crop of the same coastal drawing, with one visible
  fold and a legible folio title.
- Methods: an enlarged margin containing the five independent evidence lanes;
  their alignment does not suggest a mandatory sequence.
- Source adapter documentation: a documented source fragment with a blue
  annotation line leading to its interpretation.
- Social releases: a new crop or small illustration from the same atlas,
  paired with one actual improvement and an edition/date.
- Contributor acknowledgments: a colophon at the edge of the sheet, built
  from real contributor names when authorized for publication.
- Small assets: corner folds, reference numerals, a distinct annotation line,
  measured scale marks, and ink drawing fragments.

**Tone and provisional copy**

“A shared record of this place.”

“Follow the public evidence behind the Palisades rebuild.”

The voice is observant and generous. Captions can notice small details in the
illustration, but factual claims keep their source and date. The pleasure is
the pleasure of looking closely.

**Why it is specific**

The Palisades setting is the subject rather than a footer. Marginal references
make evidence traceability part of the visual identity. Different crops can
tell different stories without changing the core image family. This fits a
project about one place and the public record around it.

**Risk and the design response**

A beautiful atlas could be mistaken for the actual recovery map or imply
complete knowledge. Keep the illustration visually distinct from product
screens and label its role in the design study. The eventual product demo
should identify its snapshot. Avoid fake coordinates, fake source citations,
and plausible-looking household details used as decoration.

### C. The Record Machine

**Unexpected direction.** A small mechanical instrument makes the different
dates inside a public record visible and oddly satisfying to inspect.

**The opening scene**

A front-facing white instrument spans the page like a public reading-room
fixture. Three cobalt windows are labeled **Event**, **Observed**, and
**Published**. Each contains a different date on a rolling paper band. One
continuous line connects the windows to a source slip extending from the side.
The lower edge has a brass-free, unbranded mechanical lever and a small
perforated annotation strip. The main type is large, functional, and openly
numerical. The scene is precise enough to feel designed for a particular job.

This is deliberately different from the two paper-and-place illustrations.
It uses frontal symmetry, physical timing, and mechanical typography. Its
protagonist is the behavior of the record. It needs no character or hero
letter.

**Art direction**

Mostly white with deep cobalt panels and dark-blue typography. Use restrained
surface wear at moving edges, simple fasteners, and mechanically plausible
tracks. Avoid glass, luminous particles, hovering plates, generic holograms,
or a shiny consumer-electronics render. A graphic cutaway of the instrument
can work just as well as a physical model.

**One visitor action**

The visitor advances one lever or presses an equivalent labeled control. A
newer public observation arrives, but the event date on the original record
does not move. The publication band then advances to include it. A short
caption explains the difference. The example remains explicitly fictional;
there is no live countdown and no prediction of construction completion.

The motion should take roughly a second, then stop. No sound is necessary.
The static state contains the whole relationship, and the control has a
plain-language accessible description.

**Recurring scenes and motifs**

- README: a compact three-window illustration with the source slip still
  connected, beside a concrete explanation of the project.
- Release cards: an edition band representing the actual software or data
  release. A date does not stand in for freshness of all underlying records.
- Corrections: the instrument preserves a visible earlier annotation while
  attaching a new one.
- Developer documentation: diagrams of record interpretation, source bytes,
  and publication as parts of the instrument, with accurate technical labels.
- Small assets: a perforated strip, three aligned date windows, the repeated
  annotation notch, and a line that never detaches from the source.

**Tone and provisional copy**

“The date is part of the story.”

“Public evidence for the Palisades rebuild, with its source and history.”

The enjoyment comes from a precise mechanism making an initially confusing
idea clear. It can have tiny mechanical eccentricities, but the record
behavior must stay correct.

**Why it is specific**

OpenPali explicitly distinguishes event, observation, and release dates. It
retains evidence over time. This direction turns that unusual and important
product commitment into an image people can remember. Its interaction would
need substantial rewriting to advertise an unrelated developer tool.

**Risk and the design response**

It could feel cold, bureaucratic, or overly technical. Keep the main
Palisades-purpose sentence prominent and use ordinary terms beside the
instrument. It could also imply a countdown or a single official timeline.
Separate the three dates visually and show preserved history. Test whether a
newcomer can explain the difference after using it once. If they cannot, the
mechanism is adding work rather than clarity.

## Comparison and recommendation

| | Shared Table | Folded Coast | Record Machine |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First impression | A place to join | A place to understand | A record to investigate |
| Main subject | A shared activity and recurring character | The Palisades and its annotated record | Evidence history and different dates |
| Composition | Inhabited, oblique, informal | Horizontal, flat, editorial | Frontal, structured, mechanical |
| Distinctive action | Catch a source-linked sheet | Unfold a claim's reference | Advance observation without rewriting the event |
| Main source of fun | Character behavior and working details | Discovery through close looking | A satisfying, understandable mechanism |
| Strongest use | Community, repository, contribution | Website, story, methods, social | Developer introduction and evidence explanation |
| Main danger | A mascot attached to generic scenery | Attractive fictional cartography | A clever instrument that feels like a tutorial |

**Develop Shared Table first.** It best answers the desire for a project with
warmth, recognizable recurring scenes, and room for community participation.
Its success depends on the shared activity being visible. The bird should be
evaluated as a character in that activity before becoming an avatar.

Develop Folded Coast as a genuinely separate art study. It is the strongest
option if the final project should foreground place and editorial attention
over a mascot. Develop Record Machine to test a more unexpected identity
rooted in a real product distinction.

A proposed Community Print Workshop would overlap heavily with the paper,
craft, and collective activity already present in the first two directions.
It could also suggest that OpenPali manufactures records. Keep printmaking as
a possible illustration technique; use the third study to ask a different
question.

## Concrete requirements for the next art studies

For each direction, produce one complete scene and two smaller continuations
of that scene. Showing three different jobs is the best early test of whether
the idea has an identity beyond its hero image.

1. **Hero:** enough visual specificity that removing the wordmark still leaves
   a recognizable civic-record subject. Main description and GitHub action
   must remain readable at phone size.
2. **README image:** the same world can explain a different project entry point
   without a new visual style. Pair its image with ordinary linked text so
   the asset does not become a tiny, unreadable infographic.
3. **Social image:** a small moment from the same world, recognizable as a
   thumbnail. No fake adoption statistics or synthetic testimonials.
4. **Small identity:** verify that a selected recurring feature remains
   legible at avatar size. This can be developed after the scene, rather
   than turning it into the hero by default.
5. **Motion sketch:** demonstrate the single meaningful visitor action using
   three still frames before engineering it.

Judge the art before rewarding the technology. Ask: what is happening here,
why does it belong to this project, what would recur next month, and what
would a contributor want to share? If the answer depends entirely on the
caption, continue the art direction before building another landing page.

## Boundaries carried into every direction

- The work represents public evidence and careful contribution. It does not
  rank households or turn recovery into a completion game.
- Illustrations and demonstrations must be distinguishable from actual
  source records, real residents, and the deployed map.
- Preserve source attribution, corrections, unknowns, and the separate
  evidence lanes when they enter the visual story.
- Current licensing remains the project's own explicit decision; a public
  code repository is not itself an open-source license.
- Reuse none of Cua's character artwork, costumes, card graphics, or marks.
  Its useful example is consistency between a recognizable actor and a
  product-related activity.

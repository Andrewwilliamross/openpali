# The OpenPali field office

> Archived round-two direction. The current identity is [Signal: scattered blue squares on white](README.md).

**A place with footnotes.**

The field office is the illustrated world around OpenPali: a layered paper coastal atlas, tracing sheets, a source slip, and a small paper pelican taking an interest in the page. A blue pencil and clip make the scene feel in use. It connects a place with the patient work of understanding its public record.

“Field office” is a creative theme. OpenPali is an independent civic project; the artwork does not imply a government office, a physical service location, or official endorsement.

## What belongs in the scene

- **An atlas:** the place and its context. Folds and a coastal edge supply the large silhouette.
- **A source note:** the reason a claim can be checked. A bracket, tab, and fine connecting line keep it visible.
- **A notebook:** room for an explanation, an uncertainty, or a correction. An annotation adds context while leaving the original visible.
- **A paper pelican:** an interested participant, with a long blue beak, folded white body, blue wings, and small blue feet. It can look at a source, carry a note, or help turn a page. It does not represent a resident or tell a story about disaster.
- **White paper and blue ink:** a consistent setting for the artwork, with readable dark text beside it.

The atlas leads the composition. The bird gives the work a little life. Neither should obscure the plain purpose sentence: **Public evidence for the Palisades rebuild.**

## Three ways to help

The role illustrations correspond to actual project work. Keep their labels as readable text outside the image.

| Role | Suitable action in the artwork | Real route |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Follow a source | Examine a note and its connection to the atlas. | Source research, adapters, citations, and explanations. |
| Improve the software | Work at the notebook or arrange its tools. | Interface, accessibility, platform, tests, and documentation. |
| Question a record | Add a margin note while preserving the original. | A public source discrepancy or the deployment's report path. |

A click on an illustration is not a real contribution or correction. Real reports need the proper form, source context, and review. Keep resident contact details out of public artwork and GitHub issues.

## Drawing and layout

Use the same bird proportions, atlas folds, source tab, and line weight across the family. New poses should look like the same character working in the same place. Do not substitute unrelated birds or decorative paper objects for each release.

Let the illustration occupy a deliberate part of the page, with a quiet area for the headline and action. Keep the website's essential text in HTML and the README's text in Markdown. Crop the artwork separately for desktop, mobile, landscape social cards, and square cards; shrinking the desktop composition is rarely enough.

White backgrounds help README artwork survive both GitHub themes. Transparent role assets can sit on a white or very pale blue surface. Keep text, focus outlines, and controls above decorative layers.

### Geography and evidence

The art is an illustrative coastal setting, unless an individual asset explicitly documents a geographic source. Do not treat its folds, contours, or blue marks as parcel boundaries, property conditions, or a recovery measurement.

Actual maps need their own source, extent, dates, and attribution. The repository's approximate neighborhood assignments do not establish official boundaries. Historical imagery cannot stand in for current construction evidence. Keep those distinctions in any future illustration based on real data.

### Character boundaries

Use curiosity, care, and useful little actions. Keep the bird small enough that the place and public-record purpose remain clear.

Avoid rescue gear, victory poses over houses, damage scenes, fictional resident stories, and jokes attached to property evidence. Do not make a bird's mood indicate whether a person has rebuilt. Contribution and discovery supply the play.

## Voice

| Job | Copy |
| --- | --- |
| Main headline | A place with footnotes. |
| Plain explanation | Public evidence for the Palisades rebuild. |
| Website action | Explore on GitHub. |
| Source invitation | Follow the source. |
| Contribution invitation | Help make the record clearer. |
| Encouragement | Small fixes count. |

Keep claims specific. “No public evidence” describes what covered sources establish; it does not describe an owner's effort. Dates, unknowns, and conflicts deserve ordinary, factual language. Avoid “nothing to hide,” blanket promises of completeness, and unqualified claims that the project is live or open source.

## Where the family appears

- **Website:** one complete scene, a short purpose, and the GitHub link. Optional art controls remain local and accessible.
- **README:** an atlas banner and small role illustrations, with working setup commands and a clear contribution path.
- **Social:** one crop and one real announcement. A note motif can frame a source-method improvement or a shipped capability.
- **Terminal:** a compact, optional ASCII bird and folded sheet. Ordinary text must still name the project and its purpose.
- **Stickers:** a simple bird action, atlas silhouette, or headline. Remove tiny annotations that cannot survive printing.

## Before adding a new piece

1. State what the illustration is helping the reader understand.
2. Reuse the approved character and atlas references; retain editable sources and provenance.
3. Check the main image at full size and its smallest intended crop.
4. Keep essential text outside the raster image and check contrast on the destination surface.
5. Provide a complete static state and reduced-motion behavior for any interaction.
6. Verify that decorative marks cannot be mistaken for real evidence or official status.
